# Trust and About Content

## Why this matters

The About page is one of the most-visited pages on a small-business site, often the second page a visitor checks after the homepage, specifically because visitors are trying to decide whether to trust this business. A generic, fact-free About page actively undermines that decision at the exact moment a visitor is looking for reassurance, while a specific one with real details builds the credibility a homepage's marketing copy can't.

## Do this

### About page and founder/company story

Include real, specific, checkable facts, not generic sentiment:

- When and why the business actually started (a specific problem the founder saw, not "a passion for excellence").
- Real credentials, licenses, or certifications relevant to the industry, stated plainly, not buried.
- Actual scale/experience facts where they're genuinely impressive and true ("serving Fort Worth since 2009," "over 3,000 repairs completed"), never invented numbers.
- A real photo of the actual founder/team, not stock photography; if a real photo genuinely isn't available yet, say so and flag it as a placeholder rather than substituting a stock image that misrepresents the team.

Compare a generic version against a specific one:

> Generic: "We are passionate about providing excellent service to our customers. Our team is dedicated to quality and customer satisfaction."

> Specific: "Cool Air HVAC started in 2009 after our founder, a 15-year commercial HVAC technician, got tired of seeing homeowners overcharged for simple repairs. We still price every job the same way: a flat diagnostic fee, no upsell quotas for technicians."

### Visible contact email

Display a real, monitored email address somewhere easily found (header, footer, or dedicated contact page), not hidden behind a contact form with no visible address at all. Some visitors specifically prefer email over a form, and its absence reads as a business trying to avoid direct contact. See [`contact-page-standards`](../contact-page-standards/SKILL.md) for the full contact-page treatment and [`mobile-navigation-ux`](../mobile-navigation-ux/SKILL.md) for making it a working `mailto:` link.

### Social links

Link to the business's actual, active social profiles, not a generic set of icons linking to the platform's homepage or to `#`. If a profile is inactive or doesn't exist, omit that icon rather than linking somewhere that undermines the credibility the section is meant to build.

## Never do this

- Never write About-page copy that could be pasted onto a competitor's site unchanged.
- Never use stock photography of unrelated people as if they were the actual team.
- Never invent numbers, years in business, or credentials that aren't genuinely true.
- Never link a social icon to `#` or to a platform's generic homepage instead of the business's actual profile.

## Verification checklist

- [ ] About page contains specific, checkable facts, not generic sentiment
- [ ] Any team/founder photo is real, or clearly flagged as a placeholder if not yet available
- [ ] No invented statistics, years, or credentials
- [ ] A real, monitored email address is visible somewhere on the site, not only a contact form
- [ ] Every social icon links to the business's actual, active profile, never `#` or a generic homepage
