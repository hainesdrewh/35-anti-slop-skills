# Blog Content and SEO

## Why this matters

An empty or near-empty blog section is worse than no blog link at all; it signals a site that was set up and abandoned. Blog content, done well, is also one of the more effective ways for a business to rank for the specific, longer-tail questions its actual customers search for, questions a homepage or service page won't naturally cover in depth.

## Do this

### Launch with real starting content, not an empty section

Don't publish a "Blog" nav link pointing to an empty or single-post section. A reasonable baseline before launch is around five genuinely useful posts, enough to demonstrate the blog is real and to give a new visitor something to actually read, not an arbitrary quota to hit with filler.

### Write content that answers a real question

Each post should target a specific, real question or need the business's actual customers have (see [`project-scoping-and-prd`](../project-scoping-and-prd/SKILL.md) for the ICP this should be grounded in), written specifically enough that it couldn't be published unchanged by a competitor in a different city or industry.

- Good: "How Often Should You Replace Your AC Filter in Texas Heat?" (specific, locally relevant, answers a real question).
- Weak: "5 Tips for a Better Home" (generic, could be about anything, serves no specific search intent).

### FAQ sections

Add a genuine FAQ section to relevant pages (service pages, the homepage, or its own page) covering the actual questions customers ask before converting, not invented filler questions. This doubles as the basis for FAQ schema (see [`structured-data-schema`](../structured-data-schema/SKILL.md)), but only mark it up as schema if it's genuinely presented as an FAQ on the page.

### Related content

Link each blog post to 2-3 genuinely related posts or service pages at the end, using descriptive link text, both to keep visitors engaged past a single page and to reinforce the internal linking structure (see [`breadcrumbs-internal-linking`](../breadcrumbs-internal-linking/SKILL.md)). Avoid an auto-generated "related posts" widget that surfaces unrelated content just because it's the same content type.

### Author bios

Include a real author name and a short, specific bio on each post, ideally the actual person who wrote or is credited for the content, with genuine relevant credentials, not a generic "Content Team" byline with no further detail. This is both a trust signal for readers and, for topics with any health/finance/safety implications, a factor search engines weigh in evaluating content quality.

## Never do this

- Never launch a blog section with zero or one post; leave the nav link off until there's real content.
- Never publish generic filler content that could apply to any business in any industry.
- Never fabricate FAQ questions that no real customer has ever actually asked.
- Never use a generic, faceless "Admin" or "Content Team" byline when a real author is available to credit.

## Verification checklist

- [ ] Blog launches with a real, non-empty starting set of posts (a reasonable baseline is around five)
- [ ] Each post targets a specific, real question or need, not generic filler
- [ ] FAQ content reflects genuine customer questions, not invented ones
- [ ] Each post links to genuinely related content, not an unrelated auto-generated widget
- [ ] Each post has a real, specific author byline, not a generic placeholder
