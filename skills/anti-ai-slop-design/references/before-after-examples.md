# Before / After Examples

Concrete rewrites of the most common slop patterns. Use these as a calibration reference, not a template to copy verbatim — the whole point is that the "after" version should look different depending on the actual business.

## Hero section

**Slop version:**
> Centered layout. Purple-to-blue gradient background. Headline: "Empower Your Business With Next-Generation Solutions." Subheadline: "Seamlessly unlock the tools you need to succeed, all in one place." Two pill buttons: "Get Started" (filled, gradient) and "Learn More" (outline). A vague abstract 3D illustration floating to the right.

**Local business example (HVAC company) — after:**
> Left-aligned layout, warm off-white background. Headline: "Same-day AC repair in Fort Worth, most calls answered before noon." Subheadline: "Family-owned, licensed & insured since 2009. No dispatch fee if we can't fix it same-visit." One primary button: "Call (817) 555-0142" (tel: link, phone icon), one secondary: "Schedule Online." A real photo of the actual crew/truck, not stock photography.

**Dev tool example — after:**
> Asymmetric layout: headline + a live, real product screenshot (not an illustration) side by side. Headline: "Ship database migrations without the 2am rollback." Subheadline states an actual mechanism: "Diffs your schema against production before you run anything." One CTA: "Read the docs," no gradient, no second competing CTA.

## Feature grid

**Slop version:**
> Three columns, each: icon-in-a-purple-circle, bold title, one generic sentence.
> "⚡ Powerful Performance — Get powerful performance for your workflow."
> "🔒 Secure by Design — Your data is safe and secure."
> "📈 Scalable Growth — Scale as your business grows."

**After (same information, no filler):**
> Vary the format per feature instead of forcing identical cards. E.g., the performance claim gets a real number ("p99 latency under 40ms, measured on our own dashboard, linked here") instead of an icon; the security claim gets a specific mechanism ("Row-level security enforced at the database, not just checked in application code — see [`database-access-control`](../../database-access-control/SKILL.md)") instead of the word "secure."

## Testimonial section

**Slop version:**
> Auto-rotating carousel, three stock headshots of smiling people, invented quotes like "This product changed the way we work! — Sarah J., CEO."

**After, if real testimonials exist:**
> A static (non-carousel, since carousels have poor engagement and hide content) grid of 2-3 real quotes, full name, real company/role, ideally linked to a verifiable source (LinkedIn, G2, a case study page). See [`review-testimonials-social-proof`](../../review-testimonials-social-proof/SKILL.md).

**After, if real testimonials don't exist yet:**
> Omit the section entirely rather than fabricate quotes. A site with no testimonial section reads as more credible than one with fake ones — sophisticated visitors check.

## Pricing section

**Slop version:**
> Three-tier table, middle tier has a colored border and a "Most Popular" badge regardless of whether that's actually true, gradient "Upgrade" button.

**After:**
> Only highlight a tier if it's genuinely the best value for the target ICP, and say why in one specific sentence rather than a badge. If pricing is custom/quote-based (common for local-service businesses), don't force a SaaS-style tier table onto a business model that doesn't have one.

## Copy register

| Slop phrase | Specific alternative |
|---|---|
| "Empower your business" | State the actual outcome: "Cut invoice processing from 3 days to same-day" |
| "Unlock the power of X" | "Use X to do [specific thing]" |
| "Seamlessly integrate" | Name the actual integration and what it does: "Syncs to QuickBooks every 15 minutes" |
| "Take it to the next level" | State the specific next state |
| "Revolutionize the way you work" | Say what changes, concretely |
| "We are passionate about excellence" | A specific founding fact: "Started in 2015 after [specific problem]" |
